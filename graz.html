Two days
in Graz

Base: Radisson Hotel Graz, Annenstraße 67 by the Hauptbahnhof. The old town is a straight 20-min walk (or ~8 min by tram) down Annenstraße, so the car sleeps in the hotel garage (~€25/day) on the city day and only comes out for the gorge. Same house rules as the Salzkammergut plan: 10:00 start in town, 09:00 for the mountain day, dinner ~19:00 with a sunset run after if the mood strikes. Sunset in Graz is around 20:00 in late August.

Eggenberg, old town & the Schlossberg

Slow breakfast
Hotel buffet, or grab pastries at a bakery on Annenstraße on the way in.

10:20
Schloss Eggenberg — your museum map ↗
Your pin, promoted: the UNESCO palace with the planetary rooms — 24 state rooms of Baroque cosmology, by timed guided tour only, Tue–Sun, closed Mondays — book a slot. Before or after, the gardens, where the peacocks run the place. ~2 h, then tram 1 straight back to Hauptplatz.
Tram 1 back · ~15 min
13:00
Lunch · Frankowitsch map ↗
Graz institution: open-faced Brötchen at the counter with a glass of Styrian sauvignon. Closed Sundays — Der Steirer (Styrian tapas, everything with Kernöl) is the Sunday cover. Eating gluten-free? Skip the bread counter — the GF list further down has you covered, two of them within a 10-min walk of here.
14:15
Old town loop map ↗
Hauptplatz → Herrengasse → the Landhaushof, the Renaissance arcaded courtyard → up Sporgasse. Facades, courtyards, no tickets needed.
15:15
Doppelwendeltreppe map ↗
The 500-year-old double-spiral “stairs of reconciliation” in the Burg — free, open daily 7:30–20:00, five minutes of pure geometry and the best odd photo in town. The Dom and Ferdinand II’s Mausoleum are next door — and so is Edegger-Tax at Hofgasse 6, the 1569 bakery from the gluten-free list.
15:45
Coffee · Das Promenade map ↗
Your pin: the pavilion café at the Stadtpark edge, two minutes from the staircase — shaded terrace, good cakes, promenade people-watching. Daily from 9:00; Sundays it closes at 16:00, so flip it before lunch that day.
Walk · 15 min
16:45
Murinsel & the alien, from outside map ↗
Cross the river via the floating steel island (its little café rests Mondays) and admire the Kunsthaus from the bank — with Eggenberg taking the museum slot, the blue blob goes exterior-only, and honestly the outside is the show. Its best angle comes later, from above.
Walk · 15 min
17:30
Schlossberg & Uhrturm map ↗
Funicular or the glass lift up (a few euros), or the 260 zigzag stairs. Uhrturm, gardens, the red rooftops with the alien glowing below — the classic Graz frame.
19:00
Dinner · aiola upstairs map ↗
Right on the Schlossberg, open daily until late — book a terrace table so the sunset happens over your plates.
20:00
Sunset from the Schlossberg
Then the stairs down — or, for the record, the Schlossbergrutsche: the world’s tallest underground slide drops you through the hill in ~40 seconds. Tram or stroll back to the hotel.

Bärenschützklamm — the ladder day

★ 168 ladders & 24 waterfalls★ Hochlantsch 1 720 m (optional)
08:00
Breakfast + pack
Water is the thing today — carry ~1.5 L each (the hut on top charges for it) plus cash and sturdy shoes; the ladders stay damp with spray.
Drive · 40 min via S35
09:45
Parkplatz P1 Bärenschützklamm, Mixnitz map ↗
Full-day parking €6 (the €9 premium lot sits higher and trims the approach). Thu–Sun the Bärli-Express shuttle (€3 / €5 return, pay on board) also cuts ~20 min of the walk-in.
Walk in · ≈1 h 30
11:30
Into the gorge
Ticket hut at the entrance: €10 pp, cash or card, no booking. One-way traffic going up — about an hour of free-hanging wooden ladders and bridges (3,000+ rungs) past 24 waterfalls between 300 m limestone walls. Entrance closes at 16:00; short-notice closures in storms.
Through the gorge · ≈1 h
12:45
Lunch · Almgasthaus Guter Hirte map ↗
The hut above the gorge exit — honest alm food, cash preferred, famously stingy with free water (hence your bottles).
13:30
Pick your afternoon
Standard: down the Prügelweg back to the car — ~4.5 h round trip all in. Summit version (my pick for you): continue via the Schüsserlbrunn chapel, glued to its cliff, up the Hochlantsch (1 720 m) map ↗ — summit cross, Almenland panorama, ~2 h extra — then down to the Prügelweg.
Down · 1 h 30 – 2 h Drive · 10 min
16:45
Frohnleiten — the cute-town stop map ↗
Your “opcionális cuki város”, ten minutes from the trailhead and directly on the way home — one of Austria’s prettiest small-town squares, pastel facades stacked above the Mur. Coffee or gelato on the Hauptplatz, 45 minutes, zero detour.
Drive · 35 min + narrow hill road · 15 min
18:30
Sunset dinner · Fürstenstand map ↗
Your “naplemente spot”, upgraded to the finale: Graz’s house-mountain restaurant on the Plabutsch, rebuilt in 2024, the city 400 m below and the rebuilt lookout tower to climb between courses. Wed–Fri 10–21, Sat–Sun from 9, closed Mon–Tue. Reserve online and ask for a window or terrace table — walk-ins do get turned away, and service stretches when it’s full. The access road from Wetzelsdorf is genuinely narrow: downhill traffic yields, and the “private road” sign doesn’t apply to guests. Sunset lands ~20:00, right over your plates.
Down the hill · 25 min · headlights on
21:00
Back at the Radisson
If Fürstenstand’s closing days bite (Mon–Tue), the fallback is Landhauskeller in the vaulted rooms off yesterday’s arcaded courtyard — book — or Der Steirer on a Sunday.

Weather rule: the gorge is a morning job and closes at short notice in heavy rain, wind or thunder — if the forecast turns, swap the days or fall back to the Schöckl (Graz’s home mountain, cable car up, zero hiking) and keep the ladders for a clearer sky. Dogs are not allowed in the gorge, and it’s genuinely airy in places — gloves are a nice-to-have.

Gluten-free Graz

Graz is surprisingly good at this — there's even a 3,000-member local "Glutenfrei in Graz" community keeping places honest. The picks below are celiac-aware, not just gluten-light; ochre pins on the map. One magic word: say “Zöliakie” rather than just “glutenfrei” and kitchens switch to the careful protocol — and by law every Austrian menu marks allergens with letter codes, gluten is the “A”.

  • Gasthaus Lendplatzl map ↗ — the headline: nearly the whole Austrian canon gluten-free — Wiener schnitzel, Backhendl, Cordon bleu, even Kaiserschmarrn — breaded with GF flour and fried in a separate fryer; celiac in the owner's family, so they get it. 4.5★ (2,100+), Mon–Sat to midnight, closed Sun, and it's a 7-min walk from your hotel. One caution from the celiac community: ask about salad dressings (a soy-sauce one lurks).
  • Gasthaus Marschallhof map ↗ — tiny old-school Wirtshaus with its own gluten-free menu; all breaded dishes made GF on request, gourmet Cordon-bleu variations, and GF Austrian beer. 4.5★, also ~5 min from the Radisson — small room, call ahead (+43 676 7358700).
  • Freigeist Burger, Klosterwiesgasse 2 map ↗ — the street-food slot: GF buns warmed in foil, all fries (truffle, sweet-potato included) from a fryer that never sees gluten, sauces allergen-marked, plus a GF-vegan Raffaello cake. 4.5★, daily from noon.
  • AMORE LOUI Pizza Store, Schmiedgasse 21 map ↗ — GF pizza prepped in a separate area and baked on its own tray, right in the old town, ~€12–14, daily to 21:00. Handy Day-1 quick bite.
  • Hofbäckerei Edegger-Tax, Hofgasse 6 map ↗ — the pastry stop with history: Graz's oldest bakery (1569, imperial court supplier, that famous carved portal), literally next door to the Doppelwendeltreppe, and it has carried gluten-free baked goods for 20+ years. Not a dedicated GF bakery — ask which items are baked separately or sealed. Mon–Fri to 18:00, Sat to 12:30, closed Sun.
  • Maida Soulkitchen, Münzgrabenstraße 45 map ↗ — a 4.8★ living-room café on the local gluten-free lists, homemade cakes and Levantine plates; small and personal, so confirm the day's GF bake at the counter. Pairs well with Freigeist, two blocks away.

Also useful: Landhauskeller — now the Day-2 bad-weather fallback — has a long gluten-friendly reputation locally, so flag it when booking; and Café Muhr on Hauptplatz does GF cakes mid-loop, with the honest footnote that the celiac community rates its care higher than Google rates its service (3.7★, slow when busy).

Evening light & drone

Drone, short version: both headline locations are no-fly — the city centre is full of uninvolved people, and the gorge is a protected natural monument threaded with wooden structures and hikers. The one honest window is the open Almenland pasture country around the Hochlantsch shoulder and Teichalm on Day 2, away from the summit crowd — Austro Control Drone Space check before launch, as always. If you extend the drive home 25 min over the Teichalm, the moor boardwalk and rolling pastures are the couple-shot backdrop. Mini 4 Pro
  • Schlossberg / Uhrturm terraces — the Day-1 sunset spot (~20:00 in late August): red rooftops, the alien glowing blue, camera only.
  • Fürstenstand tower — Day 2’s sunset arrives with dinner: 360° from the rebuilt lookout, Graz to the east, green hills to the west. Restaurant grounds and people — camera only, drone stays down.
  • Murinsel at blue hour — lit from within, long-exposure territory from the riverbank on the walk home.

Practical notes

Closure grid to check against your actual dates: Fürstenstand closed Mon–Tue; Eggenberg’s state-room tours, the Kunsthaus and the Murinsel café close Mondays; Frankowitsch and Lendplatzl close Sundays; the gorge runs daily May 1 – Oct 31, entrance 8:00–16:00, weather permitting. A Wed/Thu or Fri/Sat pair makes every pick on this page work at once.

Book three things: a timed slot for the Eggenberg planetary-rooms tour, a view table at aiola upstairs for the Day 1 sunset dinner, and a window or terrace table at Fürstenstand for Day 2 — their online reservation works well, and walk-ins do get turned away. The gorge itself needs no reservation.

Car logistics: hotel garage ~€25/day — it only moves for the gorge. Tram tickets from machines or the Verbundlinie app; everything on the city day is walkable from Hauptplatz.

Gorge kit: sturdy shoes, ~1.5 L water pp, cash for parking/entry/hut, and a dry layer — the spray is constant and glorious.

All times approximate; the gorge entrance deadline (16:00) is the only hard one, and your 09:00 start clears it with hours to spare.
